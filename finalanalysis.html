Body Trait Preferences: Breasts vs. Buttocks

What the data says about relationship status, region, and age

This is a replication study of Mehmetoglu, M. (2026). Buttocks or Breasts? Identifying Latent Subgroups in Male Preferences for Female Bodily Features. View the original study

Summary (Plain English)

This report looks at what people prefer more — breasts or buttocks — and how interested they are in bodies in general. Here are the main takeaways:

  • Singles slightly prefer breasts over buttocks. People who are in a relationship, married, or other slightly prefer buttocks.
  • Married people are the most likely to be "Highly Body-Oriented" (very interested in bodies). People in a relationship are most likely to be "Moderately Body-Oriented".
  • The statistical model shows that the "Sexy" (valuing sexiness in a partner) factor hugely increases the chance of being Highly Body-Oriented (+229%). Being Aged 22–25 increases the chance of belonging to body-oriented groups. Being in Asia decreases the chance of belonging to body-oriented groups. Being in the Americas increases the chance of being Buttocks-Oriented.
  • Materialistic values are linked to higher interest in bodies overall. Intellectual values increase the chance of being Moderately Body-Oriented but slightly decrease the chance of being Highly Body-Oriented. Egalitarian values have little consistent effect.
  • In specific subgroups (excluding unknown regions/ages), Europeans aged 31+ show the strongest breast preference. Americans aged 26–30 show the strongest buttocks preference.
  • When we group people by their answers (profiles), Asia has a big group that likes both but leans toward breasts. Age 31+ has two big groups: one moderate with a breast lean, and one high on both with a buttocks lean.
  • Important: These are patterns in the data, not proof of cause and effect. Some groups are very small, so take those numbers with a grain of salt.

6. Caveats and Limitations

  • Small groups: Some groups have very few people (e.g., Asia N=26, Other/Unspecified N=26, one profile N=3). Their numbers can be unstable.
  • Not necessarily causal: These are patterns in the data, not proof that one thing causes another.
  • Model numbers: The percentage changes are not the same as probabilities. Large values can come from small groups or the model's starting point.
  • Many comparisons: Testing many subgroups increases the chance of finding random patterns.

7. How This Compares to the Original Study

The original study (Mehmetoglu, 2026) surveyed 395 Norwegian men (average age 22.8, mostly single). This replication uses a broader, multi-region sample of about 401 people. That difference matters: the original was one country and one gender; this study is more diverse. So we should expect some differences — and the similarities we do find are more meaningful.

7.1 Class Distribution: Similar Shape, Different Proportions

Group Original Study (Norway, N=395) This Study (approx. N=401) Comparison
Highly Body-Oriented 53% ~34% Different — the original had a much bigger "high interest" group
Moderately Body-Oriented 20% ~31% Different — more people landed in the middle here
Buttocks-Oriented 18% ~24% Different — a somewhat larger buttocks-focused group here
Body-Unoriented 9% ~10% Very similar

What this means: Both studies found the same four types of people. That is a strong result — it suggests the four-group pattern is real and not a fluke of one sample. But the exact split differs. The original Norwegian men were mostly "Highly Body-Oriented"; this broader sample is more spread out, with more people in the middle and buttocks-focused groups.

7.2 What Predicts Belonging to Each Group

Predictor Original Study Finding This Study Finding Comparison
Sexy Strongest predictor of all body-oriented groups (Highly +501%, Buttocks +349%, Moderately +189%) Strongest predictor of all body-oriented groups (Highly +229%, Moderately +83%, Buttocks +20%) Replicated — same pattern, same direction
Material Increased odds of Buttocks (+86%) and Highly (+88%); not significant for Moderately Increased odds of all three (Moderately +43%, Highly +40%, Buttocks +14%) Mostly replicated — direction is the same; here it also touched the Moderate group
Egalitarian Reduced odds of all three body-oriented groups (Highly −63%, Moderately −54%, Buttocks −52%) Weak and mixed effects (Buttocks +2%, Moderately +6%, Highly −6%) Not replicated — this study did not find the same negative link
Intellectual No association with any class Positive link with Moderately Body-Oriented (+33%); no link with the others Partly different — this study found one small effect the original missed

What this means: The two strongest findings from the original study — that valuing sexiness and valuing material things push people toward body-oriented groups — held up well. The weaker findings did not. In particular, the original study's claim that egalitarian values make people less body-oriented was not supported here. That could be because the samples are so different (Norwegian men vs. a broader group) or because the effect was never very strong to begin with.

7.3 Breasts vs. Buttocks: The Central Question

Finding Original Study This Study
Overall trait means Buttocks (3.91) rated higher than breasts (3.37) Buttocks rated higher in every relationship group except Singles (who lean breasts by +0.11)
A distinct Buttocks-Oriented class? Yes — appeared in every model tested (1 to 6 classes) Yes — appears in every relationship group (roughly 23–31% of each)
A distinct Breasts-Oriented class? No — breasts always appeared together with high buttocks ratings Partly yes — breast-leaning profiles appeared in the Asia and Age 31+ subgroups
Correlation between the two traits r = .41 (moderate positive) Not directly reported, but the same pattern is visible: people who rate one high tend to rate the other high too

What this means: The original study made a bold claim: that buttocks are the "primary" trait and breasts are "complementary" — because no group ever preferred breasts without also liking buttocks. This replication mostly supports that, but adds a wrinkle. Yes, buttocks-focused groups are stable and easy to find. But this broader sample also reveals breast-leaning profiles in specific places — especially older Europeans and young Asians. That suggests the original's "buttocks first" conclusion may be specific to young Norwegian men, not a universal rule.

7.4 Bottom Line

  • What replicated well: The four-class structure, the importance of "Sexy" as a predictor, the link between materialism and body orientation, the stability of a Buttocks-Oriented group, and the general tendency for buttocks to matter more than breasts.
  • What did not replicate: The strong negative effect of egalitarianism, and the claim that no breast-oriented group exists.
  • Why the differences? The original was 395 Norwegian men from a university; this study spans multiple regions and age groups.
  • Overall: The core findings of the original study hold up. The four "types" of body preference are real and appear in both samples. The details — how big each group is, and which values push people into them — shift depending on the sample.

Note: This comparison is limited by differences in sample composition, size, and measurement. Direct numerical comparisons should be read with care.
